# Future Work: Iowa Stream-Nitrate Virtual Sensor

**Decision document synthesizing eight research angles (A1–A8).**
Date: 2026-07-21. Target of every judgement below: an arbitrary Iowa map pin, basin delineated on the fly via USGS NLDI, weather + land use only, **no nitrate history at the target, no discharge gauge, no guaranteed monitored upstream neighbour.**

Current honest baseline (leave-one-basin-family-out, LOFO, 19 independent basin families):
CLF AUC 0.820, PR-AUC 0.634 (2.41× lift over base rate 0.263), recall 0.524 @ 10% FAR, MCC 0.466, Brier 0.137. REG R² 0.328, RMSE 4.36 mg/L.
**within_r2 0.42 vs between_r2 0.21; macro_auc 0.90 vs between_rate_r2 0.24.** A good timer, a bad site-ranker.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**Read the evidence caveat first: of 526 claims extracted across eight angles, only 37 were ever eligible for adversarial verification and only 30 survived. The other 489 are UNVERIFIED — not judged weak, just never checked, because verification was cut for budget. Two entire angles (A5 network topology, A8 target methodology) contain zero verified claims.**

With that stated: **stop engineering features from weather and start acquiring COMID-keyed static basin attributes.** The single strongest verified result in the corpus (Pool et al. 2021, 671 CAMELS catchments, spatial holdout) says the *rule* for exploiting a fixed attribute set is saturated — proximity 0.79 R_NP vs attribute-similarity 0.78, a wash — but the paper never varies the attribute *set itself*, and its fixed vector contains no drainage, subsurface or agricultural-management descriptor. That is the unexhausted lever, it is n=19-affordable (one static column, not a 300-basin architecture), it is computable at any pin from the COMID the pipeline already returns, and it is the only feature class that has ever won on this project.

**The project brief's statement that tile drainage has "NO data source identified yet" is factually wrong and is corrected by four independent angles.** At least four free, static, CONUS/Midwest-wide, pin-computable tile layers exist; the cheapest (`TOT_TILES92`) was verified end-to-end on an Iowa basin at a 110,403/110,403 COMID match rate. Build it — but temper the prior hard, because the best *calibrated* estimate of tile's effect (SPARROW Midwest TN, verified) makes it the **weakest significant** land-to-water term in the model, not the dominant control the brief assumes.

**Fluid dynamics does not help, and now we know why with a verified sign.** Advective routing already failed; the mass-balance side fails independently because Iowa nitrate is *mobilizing*, not diluting (median C–Q exponent b = **+0.61** measured on this project's own 20 usable gauged sites). A `C = load/Q` dilution denominator encodes the opposite of the observed physics.

**Two cheap non-feature moves may be worth more than any of it.** (a) Adopt a reporting discipline that brackets LOFO between an explicit random floor and a site-calibrated ceiling and reports between-site terms with intervals over repeated family partitions — at 19 units a point estimate is not decision-grade. (b) Consider **changing the target to a site-level exceedance rate**, which is (unverified) what Iowa DNR's own 303(d) methodology scores, is evaluable at 19 units rather than 160k rows, and attacks the between-site term by definition rather than by proxy.

### R1 — Tile-drainage fraction as a single static basin scalar

**What it is.** One time-invariant column per basin. **The brief's "no data source identified yet" is false** and is corrected independently by A1, A2, A3, A4 and A6.

**Why it should move `between_r2` specifically.** It is a purely static, basin-integrated, between-site quantity with zero temporal content — it can only load on the between-site term, and it is the highest-prior attribute absent from the 68-feature set. The verified Pool et al. refutation (A1 R4) establishes the logic: that study varies only the *donor-selection rule* over a fixed attribute vector (area, aridity, precipitation seasonality, snowfall fraction, wetland fraction, clay fraction, forest fraction) — a vector containing **no drainage or subsurface descriptor** — so it says nothing about the value of a better attribute, only about a better rule for a fixed one. Attribute-based selection alone still bought +0.09 R_NP over random donors, ~70% of the entire realizable no-local-data gain.

**How to incorporate — concretely.**

*Primary route (cheapest, verified end-to-end):*
```
tot_tiles92 = TOT_TILES92 at the basin outlet COMID
```
One hash lookup. `TOT_` is **already accumulated over the full upstream network**, so an arbitrary pin needs only NLDI → outlet COMID → dictionary lookup. No basin decomposition, no zonal statistics, no raster processing. Source: `TILES92_CONUS.txt` inside ScienceBase item `57067469e4b03f95a075ad3e` (parent DOI 10.5066/F7765D7V). 2,699,864 CONUS rows, percent of catchment area drained, `NODATA` identically 0.00% throughout, `TOT_TILES92 > 0` for 2,488,931 COMIDs. **VERIFIED** including an end-to-end Iowa test (Skunk R. at Augusta: 110,403/110,403 COMIDs matched, outlet `TOT_TILES` = 40.80%).

*Cross-check arm (independent lineage and vintage):*
```
tile_frac_ag = AgTile-US cells==1 within basin / agricultural (unmasked) cells within basin
tile_frac_basin = AgTile-US cells==1 within basin / total basin cells
```
**Build and test both denominators — they are not interchangeable** (A2 F8, verified). Non-agricultural land is *masked out*, not coded 0. `tile_frac_ag` is drainage **intensity** — the physically meaningful control. `tile_frac_basin` conflates drainage with crop cover already captured by the 24 CDL features. **Never take the denominator against CDL crop-cell counts** — AgTile's mask is NLCD-2016, a different product which underrepresents CONUS cropland by −18.15%. Prefer `tile_frac_ag` first on hydrologic reasoning; spend the second column only if the first shows signal.

*Third arm (already inside SPARROW):* `tiles13_perc2` from `mw_sparrow_model_input.txt`, area-weighted by `IncAreaKm2` over the upstream COMID set. **VERIFIED** (A6 F1). Circa-**2012**, not 2013 — the "13" is a naming artifact (A6 R10, corrected). Use the **raw** column and do your own transform: `ltiles13_perc2` maps zeros to exactly 0.0, **which collides with the legitimate ln(1%) = 0**.

**Enter as a TIME-INVARIANT column only. Never broadcast per-day.** That construct is exactly what produced the −0.166 N-surplus failure. The situations differ in kind — surplus is a genuinely time-varying flux frozen at one year; tile infrastructure is inertial on decadal timescales, so a 2012/2017 constant is defensible across 2008–2026 (A2 F10, verified; the previously circulated quote *"does not represent spatial or temporal changes in drainage installation"* is a **fabricated quotation** and must not be reproduced — the paper says *"The data deliver a static representation of U.S. tile drainage for the year 2017."*).

**Do NOT split into the 3 distance buckets on the first pass.** The diagnosis is a basin-integrated level deficit, not a spatial-structure deficit, and bucketing triples the column count against 19 independent units. The riparian precedent (CLF +0.039 PR-AUC, REG worse) shows bucketing static geography is not reliably free.

### R3 — SPARROW Midwest 2012 per-reach TN yield as a between-site level prior

**What it is.** Join the NLDI-returned upstream COMID set to the free USGS SPARROW Midwest data release and derive at most three static per-basin columns.

**Why it should move `between_r2` specifically.** SPARROW is a steady-state **long-term mean-annual** model with *no time dimension whatsoever* — it can only contribute to the between-site term. It is the mass-balance half of the network question delivered as a joinable number rather than a model to fit, and it encodes routed upstream sources minus attenuation: precisely a between-site absolute-level prior.

**How to incorporate — concretely.**
```
sparrow_inc_tn_yield        = sum(il_tn) / sum(IncAreaKm2)   over upstream COMIDs   [kg/km²/yr]
sparrow_dominant_src_share  = (il_tn_fert + il_tn_man) / il_tn, aggregated basin-wide
sparrow_del_frac            = DEL_FRAC at the outlet reach
```
Filter `comid > 0` and `IncAreaKm2 > 0`. Broadcast each as a **time-invariant** value across all site-days.

**Evidence and its tier.**

- Dataset facts: **VERIFIED**. `mw_sparrow_model_output_TN.txt`, 493 MB plain CSV, **1,379,904 rows × 40 columns**, one per COMID; accumulated load `al_tn` and incremental `il_tn` each decomposed into seven source shares (wwtp, fert, man, atm, urb, nfix, can) with per-reach standard errors, plus `DEL_FRAC`, `SE_DEL_FRAC`, `res_decay`. Free, unauthenticated, no rate limit. DOI 10.5066/P93QMXC9.
- **The headline fit statistics are a trap, and this is VERIFIED.** R² = 0.950 (log accumulated load), Yield R² = 0.913, conditioned RMSE 0.487 ln-units (51.8% real space), unconditioned RMSE 0.555 ln-units (60.0%). These are **pure in-sample calibration statistics** — the same 1,334 sites' measured loads estimated the coefficients, and an exhaustive read of all 88 PDF pages confirms **no cross-validation, no leave-one-basin-out, no independent test set anywhere.** Four independent reasons they are non-comparable to LOFO 0.820: no spatial holdout; different response variable (mean-annual TN **load**, kg/yr, base year 2012 — not daily-max nitrate concentration exceedance); no time dimension at all; and Yield R² is cross-sectional across a continental domain (Dakotas to Ohio) where yields span orders of magnitude, whereas `between_r2` is across 19 Iowa Corn Belt families with a severely restricted range. **Never quote them next to 0.820.**
- **Only unconditioned predictions apply at a pin.** "Conditioned" predictions substitute *observed* upstream station loads — a gauged-upstream-neighbour protocol that does not work for this product. The public release ships **only** unconditioned. Even 0.555 ln-units remains in-sample (it removes upstream conditioning but not calibration-set membership) — it is a **lower bound** on true ungauged error.
- **Accuracy degrades exactly where map pins land.** Unconditioned TN RMSE degrades from **0.39 to 0.65 ln-units** for loads below 14 ln-units. The report states *"most of the sites used to calibrate the SPARROW models were located on medium to large streams and may not represent the smallest streams"* and *"caution should be taken when interpreting the finest scale model results."* **CORRECTION:** the widely-quoted *"uncertainty ... is expected to increase for drainage basin sizes smaller than those of stream monitoring sites"* sentence is **NOT in SIR 2019-5114** — the word "uncertainty" appears once, in a reference title. It is mapper metadata boilerplate. **CORRECTION:** the "5% of calibration sites below ~31 km²" statistic is **TOTAL PHOSPHORUS ONLY**; the TN equivalent is unreported, so **TN small-basin coverage is unquantified in the source.**
- **Residuals are spatially autocorrelated (Moran's I 0.199, p=0.0005)** — i.e. the between-site level bias that is this project's core problem is present in SPARROW too, in-sample. But it is concentrated in the **arid western** study area (Missouri/Arkansas basins, the Dakotas) where site density is lowest; Iowa sits in the eastern Corn Belt where density is **highest**, and no Iowa bias is documented (A6 R5, corrected — the original claim overreached).
- **Circularity is real but bounded — VERIFIED, and the initial alarm was overstated (A6 R2).** Load-substitution is applied during *calibration* and to the calibration RMSE, **not** to distributed reach predictions. Output files contain no station ID, no measured load, no residual. A reach's published value is a deterministic function of catchment attributes and ~14–15 globally shared coefficients, computed identically at monitored and unmonitored reaches. A single Iowa site's leverage is ~1/1,334. Five Iowa providers *are* listed among the 71 contributing agencies (IA DNR, IA DMWW, IA GSWSP, IA ISUCOE, IA VWMP). **Run the audit once** — join the 85 sites against the calibration-site table by COMID **plus a distance tolerance** (the report permits a WQ site up to 40 km from its paired gauge, so COMID-exact matching will under-detect), and report how many **families** (not sites) contain a calibration site. At 19 folds, 3–4 contaminated families warrants a caveat. Report LOFO with and without those families and treat the gap as optimism bias. **The leak is evaluation-side only** — SPARROW output is defined at every COMID, so even under full contamination the feature remains usable in the shipped product.

**Feasibility at n=19.** Moderate. Attaching is trivial; **detecting** a gain is not. Under LOFO these collapse to at most 19 distinct effective values. **Pre-register a <0.03 PR-AUC LOFO gain as a null.** Fix the column count a priori.

**Risks.** SPARROW predicts **total** nitrogen; this project predicts **nitrate**. Deriving a nitrate-relevant feature requires assuming the nitrate:TN fraction is stable across Iowa tile-drained basins — **no source in this corpus quantifies it or tests its spatial stability** (A6 uncovered). If it varies systematically between basins it introduces exactly the between-site error the feature is meant to reduce.

### R4 — Reframe the target to a site-level exceedance rate

**What it is.** Predict, alongside or instead of a daily probability, a **site-level exceedance rate** — and evaluate against the regulator's own three-class bins.

**Why it should move `between_r2` specifically.** This is the **only** recommendation in the corpus that attacks the between-site gap by changing the *target* rather than by finding new features, and seven feature-engineering ablations have already failed. UNVERIFIED (A8 F1/F2): Iowa DNR's 2020 assessment methodology reportedly requires that *"significantly more than 10% of the samples violate Iowa's Class C criterion for nitrate"* over a **three-year** window, tested with a binomial small-sample rule (Lin et al. 2000) at >90% confidence, with a **three-class** outcome — Fully Supported `<10%`, Partially Supported `>10% to <25%`, Not Supported `>25%`, minimum 10 samples. If that holds, **the project has been optimizing a day-level functional while the decision-relevant functional is precisely the between-site quantity it predicts worst.**

**How to incorporate — concretely.**
1. Compute each family's observed annual (and 3-year) violation rate. Regress it on static attributes under LOFO. Report against the current `between_rate_r2` = 0.24. This is a 19-point regression on a **much less noisy target** than a daily one — that is the trade.
2. Score the model on whether it places an unseen basin family in the correct **<10% / 10–25% / >25%** bin: a 19-unit, three-class confusion matrix, computable **today** against existing LOFO folds with no new data. Report with **exact binomial intervals** — a single misbinned family moves accuracy by ~5 points.
3. Apply the Lin binomial rule to weight or exclude short-record sites when fitting the between-site rate term: reportedly n=10 needs 3 exceedances (CL 0.930), n=25 needs 5 (0.902), n=50 needs 9 (0.942), n=100 needs 15 (0.927) — an effective required fraction of ~30% at n=10 falling to ~15% at n=100. Sites with short records carry rate estimates **the regulator itself would refuse to act on.**
4. Run a threshold ablation at **5, 8, 10 and 12 mg/L**, reporting `between_r2` and `between_rate_r2` at each. 5 mg/L is (unverified) a citable regulatory boundary — 40 CFR 141.23(d)(2)-(3) triggers a monitoring-frequency change at 50% of the MCL — and would raise positives per family, tightening each family's rate estimate.

**Evidence and its tier.** **UNVERIFIED throughout. A8 contains zero verified claims.** The regulatory quotations came from a mirror, not the authoritative eCFR, and the underlying Iowa administrative code (567 IAC Ch. 61, Table 6) was **never retrieved**. **Verify before asserting this framing anywhere.**

**Feasibility at n=19.** **Uniquely high — it is the only recommendation whose evidence requirement shrinks rather than grows under the constraint.** Evaluating a predicted site-level rate needs 19 rate estimates, one per family, not 160k row-level predictions. n=19 is the *natural* sample size for this target rather than a handicap imposed on it.

**Risks / caveats that must travel with it.**
- **Legal-framing risk, and it is a legal misstatement risk rather than a modeling error.** UNVERIFIED (A8 F3): the 10 mg/L criterion reportedly applies **only to Class C** ("source of a water supply") waters in Iowa, with the criterion listed as *none* for all six aquatic-life classes and for Class A and Class HH — *"At concentrations seen in surface waters, nitrate is not believed to be toxic to aquatic life."* Separately, the federal MCL is reportedly defined on **treated** water at the distribution entry point (40 CFR 141.23(a)(2)), so applying 10 mg/L to raw in-stream water is an **analogy**, not a regulatory exceedance. **Verify Iowa Table 6 and the CFR on eCFR before choosing any public-facing wording,** and surface `is_class_c` per pin in the UI.
- The base rate is **not** comparable to published regulatory exceedance rates: reportedly 4 grab samples/year (surface water) regulatory frequency vs ~365 daily maxima, a ~90× mismatch. Measurable directly by subsampling the sensor records to a quarterly schedule.
- Distributional / EVT reformulations will **not** help. See §7 and the do-not-do list.

### R5 — Manure-N as a static basin scalar, separate from fertilizer-N

**What it is.** One static column encoding the basin's **manure** nitrogen input rank, entered separately from commercial fertilizer.

**Why it should move `between_r2` specifically.** UNVERIFIED (A2 F20, project-internal computation over files **already on disk** at `experiments/erin/data/N-P_from_*.xlsx`): for Iowa 2017, cross-county coefficient of variation is **1.029 for manure N vs 0.404 for fertilizer N — 2.5× more between-unit spread** — with a Spearman correlation between the two of only **0.611**, so they should enter as separate features rather than a single "total N input". Spending scarce between-site degrees of freedom on the variable with 2.5× the between-unit variance is sound in principle.

UNVERIFIED corroboration (A2 F18, Jones et al. 2019, **n=9** western-Iowa watersheds): 2017 flow-weighted-average NO₃-N correlated significantly with **manure N alone (p<0.01)**, with surplus N (p<0.01) and with commercial+manure (p<0.01), but showed **no significant relationship with commercial fertilizer N alone** — verbatim *"The FWA concentrations did not correlate with commercial N (p > 0.10)"* — while corn+soybean area fraction was the **weakest** significant predictor at only p<0.05. Reported mechanism: commercial application rates were near-uniform across basins (Rock 116, Floyd 112, nine-watershed average 117 kg/ha) despite FWA nitrate spanning 4.9–16.2 mg/L, so **crop area structurally cannot discriminate basins whose loading differs through the manure term.** Animal-unit density 0.45–5.00 AU/ha; the two highest-density basins (5.00, 3.53) were also the two highest-nitrate (11.5, 16.2 mg/L) vs 7.3 mg/L for the other seven averaging 0.84 AU/ha.

**How to incorporate — concretely.** Best pipeline fit (COMID-keyed, no county-to-basin reapportionment):
```
confined_manure_n_tot = TOT_ confined-manure-N at outlet COMID / basin area   [Sekellick & Sherr 2024, DOI 10.5066/P9WDBIXC]
```
Fallback from files already on disk:
```
manure_n_kg_per_km2 = area-weighted intersection of county Total_N_kg against the basin polygon / basin area
                      (use a mean over 2002/2007/2012/2017 rather than 2017 alone, to express stable RANK)
```
Optionally `hogN_frac` — hogs are reportedly 54.0% of Iowa manure N (cattle 39.7%, poultry 5.5%). Drop the nonfarm/urban fertilizer sheet (reportedly 0.56% of farm N). **Time-invariant column only.**

**Prefer manure over fertilizer, and here is the verified-shaped reason.** The Falcone **2017 fertilizer** column is **random-forest model output**, not measurement — AAPFCO 2017 state tonnage was never completed. Its 11 predictors include **annual precipitation, ET, and county corn percent**, creating direct collinearity with this project's existing weather and CDL features; county fertilizer **expenditure in dollars** dominates the model (%IncMSE 73.3, ~2× the next predictor), so the 2017 fertilizer field is substantially a re-expression of census dollars. Its OOB r² = 0.948 is a **random resample over county-years, explicitly NOT a spatial holdout.** Manure values are head-count × coefficient arithmetic and do not have this problem. (All UNVERIFIED — A2 F21.)

**Feasibility at n=19.** Good: one static column, same shape as tile. The evidence base (n=9) is *smaller* than 19 families — this is not a large-sample result failing to transfer down; it is a small-sample result that may not replicate at all. Cheap to test, expect wide uncertainty.

**Risks.** County resolution: a 1,000–2,000 km² Iowa basin typically intersects 2–5 counties, so this is **one basin-level scalar, not a per-distance-bucket feature**, and small basins may be dominated by one county. USDA **withholds** county livestock counts for disclosure — withheld hog counties grew 26 (1974) → 212 (1992) → 576 (2012), with 515 backfilled from prior censuses in 2017, **concentrated in exactly the high-confinement counties that matter most for Iowa nitrate.** Falcone is an **inputs** product, not a surplus product — it does not subtract crop uptake, so it is **not** a drop-in replacement for gTREND surplus.

### R6 — Reporting discipline (free, do it this week)

**What it is.** Four measurement changes, no modeling change.

1. **Bracket every result between an explicit lower and upper benchmark.** **VERIFIED** (A1 F5): this is the discipline Pool et al. model well. Alongside LOFO AUC 0.820, report (i) the base-rate/random lower benchmark (0.263) and (ii) an upper benchmark from a model trained *with* each held-out family's own history, then express transfer skill as **the fraction of that spread recovered**. In the source, the spread was 0.69 (random) → 0.89 (in-sample local calibration) with realizable transfer at 0.79, i.e. **transfer recovers ~50% of the random-to-local-calibration spread.** At n=19 this also makes the wide uncertainty visible rather than hidden.
2. **Report between-site terms with an interval over repeated family partitions, never a point estimate.** UNVERIFIED but directly on point (A1 F13, Parajka et al. 2013 meta-analysis of 34 studies / 75 results / 3,874 catchments): *"the 21 studies with less than 20 catchments have the largest scatter in the performance because of the smallest sample size"*, attributed to selection bias — *"in some of the studies with few catchments, these catchments were hand picked in terms of suitability for regionalisation."* The <20-catchment stratum reportedly spans NSE ~0.3–0.9. **Actionable regardless of verification status.**
3. **Add skill scores referenced to a climatological base rate: BSS, PSS, CSI, HSS.** UNVERIFIED (A8 F11) that this is the field standard in exceedance forecasting and that ROC-AUC/PR-AUC are *not* headline metrics there. **Our own arithmetic, not from any source:** a climatological forecast at base rate 0.263 has Brier 0.263 × 0.737 = 0.1938, so Brier 0.137 implies **BSS = 1 − 0.137/0.1938 = 0.293**. That is a **LOFO / spatial-holdout** number and must never be set beside random-split figures from the source paper.
4. **Compute calibration only on LOFO-held-out families, never pooled with in-fold predictions.** UNVERIFIED (A5 F17): in a 70-segment Delaware study under 5-fold spatial CV, 90% confidence intervals contained the observation only **~60%** of the time at ungauged locations vs **~90%** at gauged ones. Models trained on monitored sites are systematically **overconfident** when applied to unmonitored ones. Directly relevant at the β=2 operating point (recall 0.864, 59% false alarms): **the deployed false-alarm rate at real pins should be expected to be worse than the LOFO estimate, not equal to it.** Build in a safety margin.

**Additionally, adopt this hard rule now (A7 F5, VERIFIED source, our extension):** if any level/shape or concentration-duration-curve decomposition is ever trialled, **the CDC must be estimated from static attributes under LOFO.** Plugging in the target site's *observed* CDC is the perfect-information regime and will manufacture a spurious gain, because a site's observed CDC encodes exactly the between-site absolute level the model is failing at. If both are reported, label the observed-CDC variant explicitly as a **diagnostic ceiling**, never as a result.

### R7 — Fix the Iowa-clipped inner-join undercount

**What it is.** A known **correctness bug**, not an enhancement. 16.5% of grid cells lack crop/surplus data, and an inner join silently under-counts crop area and N surplus for basins extending outside Iowa — **worst for the largest, most influential multi-state basins.**

**Why it should move `between_r2` specifically.** It is a *spatially structured* bias on exactly the between-site level term, aliased onto basin size. It cannot average out; it just shifts the biggest basins.

**How to incorporate.**
- Adopt CONUS-accumulated attributes where available. StreamCat `Ws` metrics and Wieczorek `TOT_` values are accumulated on the **national** network, so multi-state basins are correct **by construction** with no work.
- Add a per-bucket `coverage_fraction` feature and use a **LEFT join with explicit NaN accounting**, so the model can learn to distrust under-covered basins rather than silently seeing a biased mean.
- Compute the diagnostic `sc_undercount_ratio = sc_ws_PctCrop × TotDASqKM / (current pipeline's crop area)` per site. **That number is worth knowing on its own** — nobody has measured how much the inner join is losing.
- `TotDASqKM` from NHDPlus VAA is the network-accumulated area, immune to the clip; `area_ratio = TotDASqKM / current basin area` is a second, free diagnostic.

**Evidence.** CONUS accumulation: **VERIFIED** for StreamCat and the USGS release. The undercount's magnitude on this project's basins: **unmeasured at any tier.** Note the fix is per-attribute — AgTile/TILES92/SPARROW fix it for **tile only**; CDL and gTREND remain truncated unless replaced.

### R9 — Static mass-balance normalizer

**What it is.** The **one genuinely untested mass-balance prescription that survives** A3, and it is not what the angle proposed.

**Why it should move `between_r2` specifically.** Under the Basu formulation the invariant quantity is the *flow-weighted concentration* `C_f = L_T/Q_T` — a **ratio**. So the mass balance predicts site-mean concentration ≈ (delivered N load per unit area) ÷ (long-term mean annual runoff depth). **The hydrologic term does not drop out; it moves into the DENOMINATOR as a long-term water-yield normalizer.** That is materially different from both "use raw N surplus and row-crop fraction" (what the model does now) and "add daily discharge" (what the angle proposed and what fails).

**How to incorporate.**
```
n_yield_normalized = (basin N surplus or row-crop fraction) / (long-term mean annual runoff or precipitation)
```
1–2 static columns. Test against a baseline **that already includes the raw unnormalized source terms**, so the normalization is what is being evaluated.

**Evidence and its tier.** The decomposition `C_f = L_T/Q_T`: **VERIFIED**. The between-site regression: **VERIFIED ABSENT.** Basu et al. (2010) is the canonical citation for "concentration = load/discharge as a stable per-catchment constant" and does assert that *"the anthropogenic drivers (e.g., land-use; nutrient inputs) are reflected in C_f"* — **but the paper never fits that relation.** There is no regression of `C_f` on N input, N surplus, land use, or specific discharge, and **no between-catchment R² appears anywhere in it.** *"No verified source reports an R² for this relation"* — that absence **is** the finding. Temper expectations for the denominator's contribution using the unverified n=32 UMRB result that log₁₀(mean specific discharge) explains R² = 0.034 of between-site mean nitrate.

**Blocker.** A3's uncovered list is explicit: **no verified operational spec for the long-term runoff/precipitation climatology denominator was obtained.** PRISM 30-year normals and Daymet are named in verifier corrections as plausible, with no resolution, coverage years, URL, access mechanism or license verified for either, and no assessment of whether a free gridded **runoff** (as opposed to precipitation) climatology with CONUS coverage exists. **Without this the feature cannot be built as specified.** NWM v3.0's 44-year record could supply a modelled mean specific discharge instead — but that reintroduces exactly the modelled-Q error the angle otherwise avoids.

### R11 — Label-quality audits

**All UNVERIFIED (A8), and internally contradictory. Run the measurements on our own data; that resolves the contradiction and makes the literature irrelevant.**

**(a) Daily-max vs daily-mean/median/duration.** Two reported findings pull opposite ways. *Against max:* two identical s::can spectro::lyser sensors installed side-by-side disagreed on **sub-daily** nitrate structure with inter-sensor Pearson r of only **0.429, 0.454, 0.813** at three sites, while the *same paired sensors* agreed at **r > 0.98 for DOC** — so the within-day component may be only weakly reproducible between instruments, and a daily *maximum* inherits instrument-specific noise a mean would average out. *For max being harmless:* compiled diurnal nitrate amplitudes are **0.01–0.15 mg N/L** in temperate/subtropical US/Spain/UK rivers, 0.4–0.6 in other UK rivers, 1.5 in a Mediterranean headwater — small against a 10 mg/L threshold — **and amplitude decreases monotonically with discharge class**, so the gap is smallest exactly on high-flow days when exceedance risk is highest. Reconciliation: amplitude may be small **and** poorly reproducible at once, making daily max *mostly harmless but never preferable*. **Both sources are four tropical Kenyan catchments. Settle it on Iowa data: compute the daily-max-minus-daily-mean distribution across the 160,074 site-days and count how many labels would flip.** Then rerun LOFO with daily-mean, daily-median, and a duration-based label. *(Note the duration label follows an Iowa DNR precedent — but for dissolved oxygen, not nitrate.)*

**(b) Turbidity-driven missing-not-at-random on the positive class.** Reported UV-Vis validity thresholds: **>80 FTU, >300–450 FTU, >1000 NTU** across three cited studies (two removes from verification). High-turbidity storm periods coincide with the runoff events that drive exceedance. **Directly testable on data already on disk** — co-located turbidity exists at **61 of 85** sites. Build `high_turbidity_day` at each threshold; test whether nitrate observations are disproportionately missing or QA-flagged then; rerun LOFO excluding those site-days. **A material move means measured performance is partly bounded by sensor validity — a ceiling no feature engineering can lift.** Establish which instrument IWQIS/USGS actually deploy first; do not assume the s::can. **Do NOT add turbidity as a model feature** — it does not exist at an arbitrary pin.

**(d) Area calibration of the operating point.** UNVERIFIED (A8 F7, Brenning et al. 2026, abstract only, Elsevier 403): instead of picking the threshold by maximizing F-β at β=2, pick it so the **predicted violation rate on held-out folds equals the estimated base rate 0.263**, then report omission vs commission balance. A few lines of code against existing LOFO predictions. Worth running as a diagnostic regardless of source verification — but **do not present it as an established improvement.**

### R12 / R13 — Lower-value, listed for completeness

**R12 — Donor-channel upgrade.** `rest_of_state_nitrate_lag1/2/3/5` is a single statewide scalar mean, identical for every target pin — the **degenerate collapse** of a QPPQ/copula donor channel, not an implementation of one. The formalism requires the donor channel to be per-donor and similarity-weighted with a rank correlation that decays with separation. Replacing it with **k-nearest-monitored-site nonexceedance transfer** (donor nitrate percentiles weighted by inter-site rank correlation or attribute/distance similarity) is deployment-legal: it consumes **other** sites' concurrent observations, never the target's. All 85 sensors are usable as nitrate donors (unlike the discharge case, where only 22 are gauged). **But it improves the TIMING channel, which is not the bottleneck.** Precondition test first, and it is cheap: **measure the empirical inter-site nitrate rank-correlation-versus-distance curve across the 85 sensors.** Streamflow has strong, slowly-decaying inter-site rank correlation, which is what makes donor transfer work; nitrate correlation is driven by local N application and tile hydrology and plausibly decays much faster. **If it decays fast, the whole donor idea is dead before any feature is built.** Use a simple parametric distance-decay with 1–2 free parameters — a kriged correlation surface is not identifiable at 19 families.

**R13 — NWM v3.0 static flow signatures.** The access facts are **VERIFIED and clean**: 44 years hourly (1979-02-01T01:00Z → 2023-02-01T00:00Z, 385,704 steps) × 2,776,734 CONUS reaches, NetCDF + Zarr, anonymous S3 (`noaa-nwm-retrospective-3-0-pds`), license *"Open Data. There are no restrictions on the use of this data."* **The join question is RESOLVED, not open**: `feature_id` metadata states verbatim *"NHDPlusv2 ComIDs within CONUS, arbitrary Reach IDs outside of CONUS"* — a single integer key lookup, no fuzzy matching, no route-link file. **Note the v3.0 bucket is not CONUS-only** (top-level prefixes `Alaska/`, `CONUS/`, `Hawaii/`, `PR/`) so all paths need the `CONUS/` prefix, and v3.0 Zarr lives at `CONUS/zarr/` inside the same bucket unlike v2.1's separate mirror.

**But do not ingest raw daily modelled discharge** — 160k rows of a temporal hydrologic covariate, the exact category that has failed seven consecutive times. If NWM is touched at all, derive **static per-basin flow signatures** from the 44-year record at the outlet COMID: `nwm_mean_specific_q` (mm/yr), `nwm_baseflow_index` (Lyne-Hollick or Eckhardt), `nwm_rb_flashiness` (Richards-Baker), `nwm_runoff_ratio`, `nwm_q10`/`nwm_q90`. 5–6 scalars, n=19-affordable.

**Expected value is LOW** — see §4. That is a feature-distribution shift **invisible in LOFO CV**. ## 4. Does fluid dynamics help?

**Direct answer: no — and the two halves fail for two genuinely different reasons, which is why "we already tried it" is not the whole story.**

### The advective half was already tried and already failed

Travel-time routing **is already implemented** — weather per distance bucket shifted by median flow distance / 2.1 m/s — and it is part of the "lagged weather hurt" failure (loso_r2 0.116 → 0.019). Independently echoed by an unverified Delaware time-delay ablation showing *"a general increase of RMSE"* as upstream-influence delay lengthened from 1 to 5 days. **Do not re-propose it without a new mechanism.**

### The mass-balance half was genuinely untested — and it fails for its own, separate reason

The commissioned hypothesis was that knowing discharge at an ungauged site converts into **between-site** skill via `C = load/Q` or `C ≈ N-input-yield / specific-discharge`. **That hypothesis is not supported by any verified evidence, and it is contradicted by the sign of the C–Q exponent.**

**This is the verified answer, and it is the crux:**

> **Iowa nitrate is MOBILIZING/ENRICHING, not diluting. On this project's OWN data, a verifier computed per-site log-log C–Q fits at the 20 usable gauged sites and found median power-law exponent b = +0.61.**

Dilution requires **b < 0**; chemostasis is **b ≈ 0**. Corroborating **UNVERIFIED** recomputation of the Marinos et al. UMRB archive: **b > 0 at 32 of 32 sites**, range +0.090 to +1.095, median +0.440 — **not a single site diluted on the daily-mean timescale.** Further unverified corroboration from 274 German catchments: 82.1% have b > 0 and only 5.8% show genuine dilution (b ≤ −0.1).

*Protocol on the b = +0.61 figure: descriptive within-site log-log regression on observed C–Q pairs, n=20 project gauged sites (collapsing to well under 19 independent families), **no held-out split of any kind.** It is a diagnostic, not a skill measure.*

**Consequence:** a feature or target of the form `C = load/Q`, or any specific-discharge **dilution denominator**, encodes a relationship whose **sign is opposite to the observed physics.** Any gain from such a feature would come from XGBoost inverting it, not from the mass balance being right.

### Three further verified/converging lines close the between-site question

1. **The between-site relation was never estimated in the source literature — VERIFIED.** Basu et al. (2010) is the canonical citation for the whole framing and does propose `C_f = L_T/Q_T` as an index of "effective biogeochemical stationarity", asserting that *"the anthropogenic drivers (e.g., land-use; nutrient inputs) are reflected in C_f."* **But it never fits that relation.** No regression of `C_f` on N input, N surplus, land use or specific discharge; **no between-catchment R² appears anywhere in the paper.** `C_f` is a per-catchment fitted slope requiring the site's own paired load and discharge record. Anyone citing Basu 2010 as evidence that `C ≈ N-input-yield / specific-discharge` is a validated between-site predictor is **over-reading a decomposition framework as an empirical result.** Its only headline R² (0.7 ± 0.2) is a **within-catchment inter-annual** regression of annual load on annual discharge, fit separately per catchment, with Q on both sides.

2. **PERFECT measured specific discharge is near-worthless between sites — UNVERIFIED but decisive if it holds.** Recomputed from the Marinos HydroShare archive, n=32 UMRB sites with perfect co-located USGS discharge: `log10(mean specific discharge)` explains **R² = 0.034** of between-site **mean nitrate** and **R² = 0.097** of between-site **fraction-of-days ≥ 10 mg/L**. Added to a %row-crop + %tile model it moves R² from **0.486 to 0.487 (Δ = +0.001)**. By contrast %agriculture alone gives R² = 0.539 and %forest alone R² = 0.550 for the same response. *Protocol: in-sample cross-sectional OLS on site-level summary statistics; NOT comparable to LOFO.* **The logic is what matters: a modelled discharge source can only add error on top of a signal that is already near zero.** And its direction is independently supported by the verified fact that Basu et al. never found such a relation to report.

3. **In the largest between-site nitrate study in this evidence set, discharge-derived descriptors helped organic carbon but NOT nitrate — UNVERIFIED.** Ebeling et al. 2021 computed mean discharge, mean **specific** discharge, runoff coefficient, seasonal ratio, base-flow index and flashiness for a 184-catchment subset. These raised **TOC** model skill *"between 11% and 37%"* (RF slope-b R²_crossval 0.28 → 0.44). For **NO₃-N**, no improvement is reported anywhere, and **not one hydrological descriptor appears in the top six of ANY of the four NO₃-N models** — topped instead by land cover, precipitation climatology, subsurface/aquifer type, slope and TWI. *Protocol: 3×-repeated 10-fold CV with whole catchments as units, but folds are RANDOM, not spatially blocked — optimistic relative to LOFO.* Absence-of-reporting is weaker than an explicit stated null, and the study contains **no** tile-drainage predictor (`drain_dens` there is stream drainage density, not artificial subsurface drainage).

### What the C–Q literature says actually sets a site's level

**The power-law INTERCEPT, not the slope — and the intercept is controlled by static land use and tile drainage, not by flow.** Marinos et al. (VERIFIED, direction only) report for the UMRB that *"tile drainage is a dominant control on C-Q dynamics, with increasing drainage density contributing to more chemostatic C-Q behavior."* The UNVERIFIED recomputation makes the separation explicit: the fitted intercept log₁₀(a) — the site's absolute level after removing flow — is **land-use controlled** (%agriculture R² 0.585, %forest 0.649, %row crop 0.513, %tile 0.251) but barely **scale**-controlled (log₁₀(area) R² 0.179); conversely CVc/CVq is a **scale** property (log₁₀(area) R² 0.412) and essentially unrelated to land use (%tile R² 0.001). **That is the redirect: the between-site level lives in static geography, which is R1/R2, not in flow.**

### Also: the "chemostasis kill switch" premise was itself wrong

**VERIFIED.** The plan was to compute CVc/CVq on the gauged sites and kill the angle if the median was well below 1. A verifier **ran it**: median CVc/CVq = **0.443** on the 20 usable gauged sites — "well below 1", which by the stated criterion would kill the angle. **Yet at those same sites log Q explains a median 50.9% of log-nitrate variance (per-site R² 0.21–0.87) with median b = +0.61.** CVc/CVq is low there because **CVq is LARGE (median 1.4)**, not because C is insensitive to Q. It is a variance ratio, not a mutual-information measure; it conflates "C is buffered" with "Q is wild." Three corrections: (a) the widely quoted **"<0.3 chemostatic threshold" is NOT a community standard** — it is 0.17 + 2 SD of *geogenic* solutes at Thompson et al.'s own 31 subwatersheds; the thresholds actually used are **0.5** (Thompson et al. 2011) or **1.0** (Musolff et al. 2015); (b) Thompson et al.'s own nitrate result is mean CVc/CVq = **0.70** (SD 0.49) in a paper with only **2** agricultural catchments; (c) Basu et al. state plainly that MARB nutrient export is **NON-chemostatic at DAILY and MONTHLY timescales** — chemostasis emerges only after **annual** aggregation, so it offers no support at this project's operative timescale.

**The angle should be down-weighted for the absence of a between-site Q-to-level relation, NOT for chemostasis.**

### Practical bottom line on fluid dynamics

- **Do not build a dilution feature or a load target.** Wrong sign, wrong identifiability.
- **Do not fit a per-site C–Q slope as a feature.** UNVERIFIED (Gorski & Zimmer 2021) but sharp: two anomalous low-flow low-nitrate events in October 2017 shifted a seasonal stormflow C–Q slope from **0.09 to 0.71 (7.9×)** despite representing *"<1% of annual flow and NO₃ load"*; an anomalous ~3-month low-flow window changed an annual baseflow slope from **0.42 to 1.42 (3.4×)**. And b is only weakly predictable from static attributes (R² 0.147–0.345), so **there is no route to a C–Q slope at an ungauged pin.**
- **NWM does not rescue it.** VERIFIED through v3.1 by direct source inspection of `NCAR/wrf_hydro_nwm_public` v5.4.0: **no tile-drainage module, no `OPT_TDRN`, no `TILE_DRAIN`/`TDRAIN` namelist option** — only generic Noah-MP `QDRAIN`. So NWM does not supply the known-missing Iowa control either. **CORRECTION:** the derived claim that this produces a *"systematic low bias scaling with tile density"* is **NOT supported and should be dropped** — in the calibrated configuration the source paper reports median event peak-flow bias **+5% (HIGH)**, reduced to +0.57% with tile drainage, and low flows **overestimated** (+50% even with the scheme); the −43% to −50% figure is **groundwater recharge**, an internal flux absent from the streamflow product; and the paper **never regresses bias against tile percentage** and cannot, because both gauge sets are pre-screened to tile fraction ≥10%, truncating the range of the exact covariate the claim needs.
- **Most important reframing:** everything NWM demonstrably gets wrong — timing, peak shape, event hit rate, flashiness — is the axis on which this model is **already strong**, while the level effect is largely absorbed by calibration (adding tile drainage changes annual volume by only **+2.3%** while changing timing metrics substantially: COR +0.15, NSE +0.16, KGE +0.22 for high flows). **NWM Q is a plausible within-site timing covariate for a model that does not need one, and a poor between-site level covariate for a model that desperately needs one.**
- **CORRECTED benchmark:** Towler et al. 2023 Central-region median KGE is **0.45** for NWMv2.1 and **0.33** for NHMv1.0 (n=1,450, Falcone 2011 aggregated **ecoregions**, not HUC2) — **not** 0.40/0.29 (0.29 is the West/NHM value); CONUS-wide NWMv2.1 median 0.53. **It is NOT a temporal holdout** — calibration periods overlap the evaluation window, so 0.45 is optimistic. Two corrections cut *against* the pessimistic reading for Iowa: the "poor plains performance" sentence names **North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska, Kansas and Texas — all WEST of Iowa**; Iowa sits in the portion the paper calls *"more mixed"* (not "most mixed", a misquote) and **no HUC-07 figure exists in the paper.** And the Central shortfall is driven by correlation (r 0.71) and variability ratio (rSD 0.87) rather than volume bias (PBIAS +4.4%) — a **timing** deficit, not a level deficit.
- **What survives:** R9 (the ratio normalizer — the hydrologic term moves into the *denominator*, and it is a **static** climatology, not a daily series) and, at low priority, R13 (static flow signatures only).

This directly parallels our within_r2 0.42 vs between_r2 0.21 diagnosis.

### A6 — Borrow a calibrated process model: SPARROW / SWAT / Iowa NRS outputs as features
**Verdict: PURSUE, BUT NOT AS FRAMED.** The **data-acquisition half is verified, cheap and deployment-compatible** and is the recommendation (R3). The **process-guided-ML half is not supported by any verified evidence in this corpus** — Read et al., Jia et al., Willard et al. all arrived at tier 3 and were never verified. Even as leads that literature points *away* from the ambitious version: the pretraining recipe is an LSTM weight warm-start that **does not port to XGBoost at all**, and its reported benefit is concentrated in **temporal** label scarcity at monitored sites — the opposite of our regime (label-rich in time, site-poor). **Do not fund a PGDL rebuild.** Budget a few days, add 3–5 hand-picked static columns, pre-register that a <0.03 PR-AUC LOFO gain is a null.

**Most valuable finding (VERIFIED, and it corrects the project's own prior):** SPARROW Midwest TN gives `ln(tile drains, % of catchment)` a coefficient of **+0.101** — statistically strong (p<0.0001, t=4.5, VIF 2.3) but a **modest elasticity**: doubling tile percentage raises modelled TN delivery by only **~7.3%**, and a 10× increase by ~26%. In the same table `ln(runoff)` carries **0.673**, `ln(soil clay)` 0.292, `ln(no-till)` 0.124. **Tile drains are the WEAKEST significant land-to-water delivery term in SPARROW's own TN fit — runoff dominates by roughly 7× in log-space elasticity.** *Protocol: in-sample calibration coefficient, n=1,334 sites, no holdout.* Note the identical variable enters the companion **TP** model with a **negative** coefficient (−0.091, p=0.0221) — the term is doing pathway-partitioning work, not acting as a universal "more tile = more nutrient" flag.

### A7 — Fix the output not the input: level/shape decomposition, duration curves, hierarchical pooling
**Verdict: PURSUE ONLY AS A CHEAP DIAGNOSTIC AND ONE NARROW EXPERIMENT; DO NOT ADOPT THE MACHINERY.** The decomposition **relocates** our problem rather than solving it — it concentrates 100% of the residual error into the site marginal, which *is* the failing `between_r2` term, and no verified source supplies a method for regionalizing that marginal from attributes at our sample size.

**Most valuable finding (VERIFIED, and it is a protocol refutation):** the flagship pure attribute-to-distribution regionalization study (Worland et al. 2019, doi:10.1029/2018WR024463 — a multioutput NN emitting all FDC quantiles from static basin characteristics) **is not spatially held out.** The authors' own code (`scripts/Worland/remake_scripts/keras_funs.R`, `sw_k_foldcv`) does `N <- nrow(data); set.seed(1); indices <- sample(1:N); folds <- cut(indices, breaks=k, ...)` — **random 10-fold CV over ROWS of a site-decade table with no grouping by `site_no`.** Each gage contributes up to 6 decade-rows with near-identical static attributes, so **the same gage appears in train and test.** That is precisely the leakage our 19-family CV exists to prevent. The literature's flagship attribute-to-duration-curve result carries **no ungauged-transfer evidence at all.** *(Also corrected: 27 quantiles, not 15; ~956 gages / ~2,804 gage-decades, not 688; the "40 basin characteristics" figure is unconfirmed and may be a transcription of the 40-unit hidden layer.)*

And its verified directional finding is a **negative for us**: *"basin characteristics are most useful for predicting smaller quantiles, whereas bias terms from neighboring quantiles are most informative for predicting higher quantiles."* Our label is an **upper-tail** exceedance.

### A8 — Exceedance methodology, and whether the prediction target itself is right
**Verdict: PURSUE, BUT NOTHING HERE IS YET CITABLE.**
> **⚠ THIS ANGLE CONTAINS ZERO VERIFIED CLAIMS.** Verification was cut before reaching A8. All 34 upstream extractions are tier 3. **Every number below must be re-verified before it enters any writeup, slide or design decision** — including the 80–98% area underestimation, the AUC 0.567/0.482 zero-shot figures, the PSS ratio 0.65, the inter-sensor r 0.43–0.81, and every CFR and Iowa DNR quotation. Two of the six sources are 2026 preprints/articles that could not be fully retrieved (Elsevier 403; arXiv figures read off log-scale plots) — exactly the profile where "confident overreach from thin evidence" bites.

**Most valuable finding (UNVERIFIED):** **the regulator's own definition of nitrate exceedance in Iowa is a SITE-LEVEL RATE, not a day-level event** — reportedly a three-year frequency-of-exceedance test binned at 10% and 25% of samples. If that holds, the project has been optimizing a day-level functional while the decision-relevant functional is precisely the between-site quantity it predicts worst. **That reframing costs no new data, is evaluable at 19 units (19 rate estimates, not 160k rows), and would survive even if most of the angle's numeric claims turn out misstated.** See R4.

**Second most valuable, and it is negative:** the methodological fork the angle was commissioned to resolve — direct binary classification vs regression/distributional-then-threshold — appears to be a **non-issue at our base rate.** The distributional route's reported advantage is concentrated at rare-event probabilities (p ≪ 0.2) and vanishes at moderate p (reported PSS₁/PSS₂ ≈ 1.0 at p=0.2, falling to ~0.65 at p=0.002). **Our base rate is 0.263 — above the regime where the paper finds any advantage.** The current direct-classification choice is fine; do not spend effort switching it.

## 7. Conflicts between angles

**These are stated as conflicts, not smoothed over. Where a tier-1 claim conflicts with a tier-3 claim, the verified claim wins — but the tension is recorded either way.**

### C1 — How important is tile drainage? (the real conflict, and it is verified-vs-unverified in *both* directions)

- **Side A (A6, VERIFIED):** SPARROW Midwest TN gives `ln(tile %)` coefficient **+0.101** — **the weakest significant land-to-water delivery term in the model**, ~+7.3% delivery per doubling, against `ln(runoff)` at **0.673** (≈7× larger in log-space elasticity). The same variable enters the **TP** model with a **negative** coefficient. A6's verdict states flatly: *"The project's belief that tile drainage is 'the dominant hydrologic control in Iowa' is not supported by this model."* **Expect a real but small between-site contribution, not a fix for between_r2 0.21.**
- **Side B (A2, A3, A4):** Pandit et al.'s Shapley analysis ranks tile drainage **first** among spatial drivers of **mean** nitrate concentration (**ranking VERIFIED from the abstract; magnitudes NOT recoverable**). An unverified recomputation of the Marinos UMRB archive gives **%tile alone R² = 0.575** for between-site exceedance-day fraction (n=32, in-sample). An unverified project-internal pilot gives r=0.516 / r²=0.266 vs site violation rate (n=46, in-sample). David et al. 2010 attributes **17%** of spatial variation in winter-spring nitrate-N yield to tile extent, *after* the runoff×fertilizer term (n=153, in-sample).
- **Side C (A2, VERIFIED, and it cuts across both):** the collinearity and error-structure objections. Basin tile fraction is approximately "flat, poorly drained cropland fraction" — a quantity the model may already carry via 24 CDL columns plus lat/lon (#2 and #3 by importance), especially since Iowa tile concentrates in the Des Moines Lobe. And errors are **county-blocked by construction**, so aggregation does not average them out.

**How to hold all three.** They are measuring different things and none is decisive. SPARROW's 0.101 is a coefficient inside a 17-variable model that **already contains `ln(runoff)`** — conditioning on runoff will absorb much of tile's effect, because tile drainage *works by changing runoff pathways*. The Shapley/UMRB results are marginal or model-agnostic attributions that do not condition on runoff. **This is exactly the difference between a partial and a marginal effect, and the corpus never reconciles it.** Also note A2 F7's countervailing verified point: AgTile thresholds are solved **separately for each county** with USDA census acreage as the target, so **basin tile fraction = f(soils, slope, cropland, COUNTY CENSUS TILE ACREAGE)** — and that last term is exogenous, farmer-reported information about human drainage investment **not recoverable from any raster currently in the feature set.** Within a county the variation is redundant; **between** counties it carries real information, and Iowa basins span many counties.

**Practical resolution:** build it (it costs one column), pre-register a **modest** expected gain rather than a fix, run the lat/lon-conditional check first (R0c), and report a null result as *"already captured by coordinates"* rather than *"tile drainage does not matter."*

### C2 — Does pooling more sites help, or hurt?

- **A4 (VERIFIED, STREAMFLOW):** Kratzert/Gauch et al. 2024 — median NSE still rising monotonically at 531 CAMELS basins with no plateau (*"even these 531 basins are most likely not to be enough"*), and training on HUC-sized subsets of **2–79 basins** is **worse** than pooling everything: *"even if enough basins exist to divide your training data into hydrologically informed training sets, one is likely to be better off simply training a single model with all available data."* **Protocol: temporal holdout at gauged basins only — the weakest possible protocol relative to LOFO, and the paper contains NO ungauged-basin experiment at all.**
- **A3/A4/A2 (UNVERIFIED, NITRATE):** Gorski et al. 2024, n=46 — pooling **hurt**: global 0.63 vs single-site 0.66 vs 5 attribute clusters 0.66 vs 8 hydro-terrane groups 0.65 (median KGE). Authors: *"there is a threshold of dissimilarity beyond which more data does not improve the model"* and *"While many deep learning studies have shown that national or even global models can outperform local models, it is not clear that this is true for water quality constituents."* **Protocol: temporal holdout, all 46 sites in training in every scheme, daily observed discharge a required input. NO spatial holdout anywhere.**
- **A5 (UNVERIFIED, STREAM TEMPERATURE):** a single pooled model beat regionally-grouped models in **14 of 17** regions and cluster models in 14 of 15, with grouped models collapsing precisely where groups were **small** (Lower Colorado, 2 training sites: regional median RMSE 6.38 °C vs 2.10 °C for the CONUS model, 3.0× worse). Also a reported "data synergy" effect: deep models *"perform better when fed a diverse training dataset spanning multiple regions… EVEN WHEN the homogeneous data is more relevant to the testing dataset."*

**A4's own adjudication, which I adopt:** *"the verified result is STREAMFLOW, the unverified one is NITRATE, and the whole point of this angle is that the two targets may not behave alike. Because Gorski is tier 3 and Kratzert/Gauch 2024 is tier 1, the verified claim wins where they overlap — but they do not overlap on target, so the honest reading is that the streamflow pooling evidence should not be assumed to carry to nitrate in either direction."**

**Practical consequence:** R8 is worth doing but is **not** a safe bet. It ships with a mandatory Iowa-only LOFO baseline (A4 F16's guardrail).

### C3 — Do static attributes beat lat/lon as model inputs?

- **A1 (VERIFIED):** as a **donor-selection rule**, attributes and proximity are equivalent — 0.78 vs 0.79 R_NP, 671 CAMELS catchments, spatial holdout (though under R_KG the spread is 0.04 rather than 0.02, so the "wash" is **metric-dependent in magnitude though not in sign**).
- **A1 F12 / A4 F9 (UNVERIFIED):** as **model INPUTS**, attributes may carry substantially more. Willard et al. 2024: meteo-only 1.77 °C → +lat/lon/elev **1.72** (Δ0.05) → +GAGES-II statics **1.47** (Δ0.25) — a ~5× larger effect, with the authors stating location information *"did not meaningfully improve performance."* Kratzert et al. 2019: adding 27 static CAMELS attributes moved median NSE **0.63 → 0.74** (+0.11, p < 1e-9) while the penalty for never having seen the basin at all was only **−0.05** (0.74 in-sample → 0.69 ungauged).

**A1's own adjudication, which I adopt:** *"These are not strictly contradictory — Pool measures a donor-SELECTION rule while Willard measures INPUT FEATURES inside one pooled model, and the two are different mechanisms — but they push in opposite directions on the angle's central question, and per the tier rules the verified claim wins where they genuinely conflict. The honest synthesis is: as a donor-selection metric, attributes and proximity are equivalent (verified); as model INPUTS, attributes may carry substantially more than lat/lon (unverified). **Our architecture is the latter case.**"*

**Note the split-protocol trap in the Kratzert numbers:** 0.63 and 0.74 are **temporal** holdout on in-sample basins (train 1981–1995, test 1996–2010); only 0.69 is spatial. **They must not be conflated.**

### C4 — Which tile product?

- **A2 F26 (UNVERIFIED) says AVOID the Nakagaki 12-state 2012 layer:** it disaggregates from **STATE**-level census acreages rather than county-level, so it carries *less* spatial information for distinguishing basins *within* Iowa despite its newer vintage than TILES92; and its 12-state extent reintroduces multi-state truncation.
- **A3 D2 (UNVERIFIED) RECOMMENDS it as a control arm:** it stores **continuous** tile-drained area in m² per cell (fraction = value/900) rather than a binary flag — *"a meaningful advantage over AgTile-US for computing a basin-mean fraction"* — and, decisively, **it is the layer Marinos et al. used**, so using it makes the n=32 UMRB %tile R²=0.575 result *directly* comparable rather than approximately so.
- **A6 (VERIFIED) endorses it indirectly:** it is the source layer behind SPARROW's `tiles13_perc2`, already pre-aggregated to NHDPlusV2 catchments — *"Do NOT process the raster."*

**Resolution:** all three are defensible for different purposes. **A2's objection is about within-Iowa discriminating power; A3's endorsement is about comparability to a specific published result; A6's is about access convenience.** Use `TOT_TILES92` + AgTile-US as the primary pair, and pull Nakagaki via SPARROW's `tiles13_perc2` (free, already aggregated) as a third arm — **disagreement among the three bounds the measurement error on the feature, which is worth knowing before attributing any LOFO change to it.**

### C5 — Is SPARROW output leaky?

- **A5 (UNVERIFIED) flags it as an unresolved blocker:** *"SPARROW's Mississippi-basin calibration stations plausibly include Iowa nitrate monitoring sites that are also in our training set… cross-check the calibration station list."*
- **A6 (VERIFIED) says the alarm was overstated and quantifies why:** conditioning is applied during calibration and to the calibration RMSE, **not** to the distributed reach predictions; the public release ships **only** unconditioned loads; output files contain **no station ID, no measured load, no residual**; per-site leverage is ~**1/1,334**; and the target quantities differ, so even a fully contaminated reach would leak a slow basin-average **annual load** level, never a daily 10 mg/L label.
- **But A6 also identifies a genuinely severe leak A5 missed:** the `flux_*_final` columns inside the **input** file. That one is real.

**Resolution: keep the audit, drop the alarm — and whitelist the input columns.** The audit is cheap insurance at 19 folds; the whitelist is mandatory.

### C6 — Is stormflow nitrate chemostatic?

- **A3 (VERIFIED, this project's own data):** median b = **+0.61** across 20 gauged sites; b > 0 at 32/32 UMRB sites.
- **A3 F22 (UNVERIFIED, Gorski & Zimmer 2021, n=5 nested Iowa watersheds):** stormflow nitrate is *chemostatic across all seasons* with event-averaged C–Q slopes distributed narrowly around zero, and tile density has *"no systematic effect on stormflow c-Q slopes."*

**A3 flags this as unresolved rather than papering over it, and I preserve that.** The verified enrichment result wins on tier rules. The plausible reconciliation is a timescale/flow-regime distinction (event-scale storm slopes vs whole-record daily slopes; stormflow vs baseflow partitioning), **but no source resolved it.** It bears directly on whether Q could matter during the specific storm windows that drive 10 mg/L exceedances — so it is not cosmetic.

### C7 — Is the daily maximum the right label?

Internal to A8, both sides UNVERIFIED, and the angle explicitly flags the tension: inter-sensor reproducibility of **sub-daily** nitrate is poor (r 0.43–0.81 vs >0.98 for DOC on the same paired instruments), which argues against a maximum; but reported diurnal **amplitudes** in temperate agricultural rivers are 0.01–0.6 mg N/L, small against a 10 mg/L threshold, **and shrink further at high flow when exceedance risk is greatest**, which argues the choice is nearly harmless. **Both come from four tropical Kenyan catchments.** Reconciliation: amplitude may be small **and** poorly reproducible at once — making daily max *mostly harmless but never preferable.* **Settle it on Iowa data (R11a); that makes the Kenyan transferability question irrelevant.**

### C8 — Is N surplus the wrong vintage or the wrong variable?

- **A2 (UNVERIFIED) says wrong vintage/wrong form:** reframe surplus as a **static basin RANK** rather than a broadcast time series, supported by reported Iowa county rank-stability (Spearman vs 2017 of 0.910–0.969 for farm fertilizer N, 0.803–0.963 for manure N, n=99 counties, 1997–2012).
- **A3 R10 says wrong VARIABLE**, from two directions. UNVERIFIED (Ebeling, n=759): N surplus correlates with mean nitrate at only **r = 0.39 (r² ≈ 0.15)** and appears in the top six predictors of **neither** the PLSR nor the RF model, while plain forest fraction alone explains **32%** and agricultural fraction **29%** — despite N surplus correlating strongly with agricultural land (r = 0.71). VERIFIED (Basu 2010): annual flow-weighted concentration in the MARB stayed **temporally invariant despite a ~1.5-fold increase in nutrient inputs over three decades**, and Baltic efforts to cut inputs ~50% *"have failed to show positive results"*, because export is *"transport- rather than supply-limited, such that contemporary distributed and point sources have lesser influence."*

**Resolution:** these are compatible and jointly informative. The static-rank reframe is cheap and worth testing; but temper the prior — **crop/land-cover fractions plus subsurface and tile attributes may simply beat an N-budget product**, and the transport-limitation result independently explains the −0.166 broadcast failure.
